M.Tech in Signal Processing and Machine Learning · NIT Jalandhar, India

I’m interested in using machine learning, computer vision, and signal processing to build AI that is actually useful to people. Healthcare is where most of my work has been so far. For my thesis, I built a model that predicts sepsis early from ICU data, which meant combining different kinds of patient data, like vital signs and lab results, in a single model. Next, I want to work with brain signals (EEG) and medical images such as CT and MRI. I’m also drawn to human–computer interaction and robotics, and I’m curious how the same ideas could be used there, for example in brain-computer interfaces or robots that respond to people. Wherever I end up, I’d like to learn how to make these models accurate and something people can trust.

Adaptive Improved Primal-Dual Distributed Stochastic Gradient Driven Distributed Active Noise Control System

A. Kar, P. K. Shill, A. S. Shamkuwar, S. Saini, Vasundhara, S. Shoba, M. G. Christensen · IEEE Open Journal of Signal Processing, vol. 7

A distributed algorithm for noise-cancelling systems with several nodes. In simulations on a five-node network it converged faster and left less residual noise than standard methods, including under impulsive noise.

Paper (IEEE)

My role: I wrote the manuscript up to the simulation section (introduction, background, proposed algorithm and performance analysis) from the methodology provided by my supervisor, and made Figures 1 and 2. Simulations and results were done by co-authors.

A. S. Shamkuwar (first author), P. Bhardwaj, N. Kumar, M. Singh · CIPHER 2026

Compares classical machine learning, recurrent networks and a multimodal attention model for early sepsis prediction on the PhysioNet/CinC 2019 data. This is the conference version; the thesis extends it with a stricter evaluation.

Spatio-Temporal Node Localization in Wireless Sensor Networks Using Hybrid Recurrent-Graph Neural Network

A. Sharma, A. S. Shamkuwar, U. Anand, R. Kumar, N. Kumar, M. Singh · CIPHER 2026

Combines graph attention networks with recurrent units to estimate positions of moving sensor nodes. In simulations, the GAT-GRU model was the most accurate overall and stayed stable under heavy noise.

Paper (IEEE)

My role: I ran the simulations and produced the results (four graph and recurrent models across 15 mobility and noise settings).

Projects

MultimodalSepsisNet: early sepsis prediction (M.Tech thesis)

PyTorch · PhysioNet/CinC 2019 · Supervisor: Dr. Manjeet Singh

Splits 40 clinical variables into four groups (vital signs, lab values, missing-data patterns, static data) and joins them with cross-modal attention. Tested on 39,721 ICU records against eight baselines, including XGBoost, with confidence intervals and ablations.

Human Activity Recognition: signal processing vs. deep learning

Python, PyTorch, scikit-learn · Code

Built 63 time- and frequency-domain features from raw accelerometer and gyroscope signals and compared Random Forest, 1D-CNN and BiLSTM. Gravity-orientation features fixed sitting/standing errors (accuracy 89% to 95%). Leave-one-subject-out accuracy over 30 subjects: 93.9% ± 5.3%.

Real-time pothole detection and geotagging

Python, OpenCV · B.Tech final-year project · Code

A real-time detector using a YOLO-tiny model in OpenCV with GPU inference. It filters false detections, saves annotated frames with location coordinates and records result video.
